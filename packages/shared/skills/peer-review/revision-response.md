# Responding to reviewers

The reviews came back. What the writer does next decides whether the paper survives.

Adapted from `peer-review` in K-Dense-AI/scientific-agent-skills (MIT). See NOTICE.

## Read all of it before answering any of it

Reviewers contradict each other. If one asks for a section to be cut and another asks for
it to be expanded, that is a decision for the writer and the editor, not something to
resolve silently by picking one.

The editor's letter outranks the reviews. If it says which points are essential, that is
the actual list of required work; the reviews are the reasoning behind it.

Wait before drafting. The first reaction to a harsh review is almost never the response
that should be sent.

## Sort every comment into one of three

**Concede and fix.** The reviewer is right. Make the change, say where it is now. Most
comments belong here, and treating them so is not weakness — a revision that concedes
nothing reads as defensive regardless of how well argued it is.

**Concede partially.** The concern is real, the requested fix is not the right one, or is
out of scope. Acknowledge the concern in the reviewer's own terms, then propose what you
did instead and why it addresses it. This is where most of the writing effort goes.

**Disagree.** The reviewer misread something, or is asking for work the paper does not
claim to do. Say so with evidence, not with irritation. If they misread it, the text was
probably unclear — so change the text too, and say that you did. A disagreement that ends
with no change at all is the hardest kind to win.

The one thing never to do is stay silent on a comment. An unanswered point reads as
evasion, and editors notice.

## How the response letter is built

One reviewer at a time, one comment at a time, in the order they wrote them.

Quote the comment, then answer it. Point to where the change is in the manuscript —
section, page, or the revised sentence quoted. A response that says only "this has been
addressed" forces the reviewer to search, and a reviewer who has to search is a reviewer
looking for reasons.

Keep each answer short. The letter is not the place to re-argue the paper.

Thank them once, at the top, and mean it. Repeated gratitude in every entry reads as
nervousness.

## What not to write

Never claim a change you did not make. This is discovered constantly, and it ends worse
than any original criticism.

Do not answer a scientific objection with a promise about future work, unless the
reviewer offered that route.

Do not describe a reviewer as mistaken, careless, or hostile in the letter, even when
they were. The editor reads everything.

## When the paper was rejected

A rejection with substantive reviews is still useful: the same objections will come from
the next venue's reviewers, so fix them before resubmitting elsewhere.

If resubmitting to the same venue is permitted, a resubmission is judged partly on how
the previous round was answered. The response letter matters as much as the manuscript.

## Terms to leave in English

major revision · minor revision · desk reject · rebuttal · response letter ·
resubmission · cover letter · reviewer · editor
